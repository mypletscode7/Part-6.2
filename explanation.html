What is the MYP?

The Middle Years Programme, an educational framework created by the International Baccalaureate (IB) for students aged 11 to 16.

The full MYP is designed as a five-year programme, known as MYP years 1 to 5. Some schools offer only part of it.

The MYP is a school programme, rather than one universal syllabus. It's a framework meant to support a curriculum.

The MYP meaning is straightforward: MYP stands for Middle Years Programme, the IB framework for students aged 11 to 16.

What Are Phases?

Phases are mainly applied to Language Acquisition. Whether you are taking German, Chinese, Spanish, or another MYP Language Acquisition subject, your learning is organized into phases. In Language Acquisition, there are three levels: Emergent, Capable, and Proficient. Your given phase is your current abilities in that language.

Emergent Phases 1-2
Capable Phases 3-4
Proficient Phases 5-6

Your phase is not a grade or a mark. It is where you are in your language that matches your abilities.

The important thing is that Phase 1 does not mean Grade 6, Phase 2 does not mean Grade 7, and so on. A student's phase is based on their language ability and development, not simply their school year. For example, two students in MYP5 could be in completely different phases of German. One might be in Phase 2, while another could be in Phase 5.

How MYP Assessment Works

Your teacher gives you a summative assignment. It might be one or more criteria.
↓
Your work is marked using one the MYP criteria for that subject.
↓
Your teacher uses the criterion descriptors to decide which achievement level from 0 to 8 best matches your work.
↓
Your achievement levels across the four criteria are added together to create a total out of 32. Your total mark will be matched to a grade level from 1-7.

Example:

Criterion A: 7
Criterion B: 6
Criterion C: 7
Criterion D: 6
Total = 26/32
Final Mark : 6/7 (no pun intended)

That total is then converted into your final 1-7 subject grade. The 6/7 was determined using this system:

Grade Total Criterion Points Performance Level
10-5Excellent
26-9Basic Awareness
310-14Developing
415-18Satisfactory
519-23Strong
624-27High Quality
728-32Excellent

In a school year, you will most likely complete the cycle of A-D at least two times.

Assessment

MYP Criteria

The main MYP subjects (Language and Literature, Language Acquisition, Individual and Societies, Science, Mathematics, Arts, Design, and Physical and Health Education) all have four criteria (A-D). Each subject’s criteria is different. For example, Science Criterion A and Language & Literature Criterion A assess different things.

Each criterion is assessed using achievement levels from 0-8. Think of the achievement level as a score from 0-8 that shows how well you did.

Your teacher looks at the criterion descriptors and decides which level best matches your work.

Your criterion level and your final MYP grade are not the same thing.

Criterion level:
0-8

Final MYP subject grade:
1-7

The total value of your marks from A to D determine your final subject grade.

So, Your subject has four criteria → you work is assessed against those criteria → you receive an achievement level → those results make your final 1-7 grade.

Achievement

Achievement Levels

For each of your subject’s criteria, there are achievement levels from 0-8. An achievement level shows what you work achieved, and is matched with a boundary: 0, 1-2, 3-4, 5-6, and 7-8.

Level What it generally means
0 The work does not meet the minimum described level.
1-2 You have demonstrated a limited level of achievement.
3-4 You have demonstrated a developing level of achievement.
5-6 You have demonstrated a good level of achievement.
7-8 You have demonstrated a high level of achievement.

To reach that 7-8 achievement level, understand what is being assessed, read each descriptor, compare and improve your work. If you really want to achieve 7-8’s, you have to go beyond the mindset of “I want a 7/8”; you need to ask yourself “What does my work require to reach the level 7-8 descriptors?”

So, within the different criterions you have achievement levels. Each achievement has different descriptors, which your teacher will use to determine your final grade. For success, really understand what your work needs to achieve to reach your desired achievement level.

Formative vs Summative Assessments : What’s the Difference?

The best way to understand the difference between a formative and summative assessment is to think of it as a school presentation. Before the presentation, you practice what you are going to say. Think of this stage as taking a formative assessment. Eventually after practice, you are ready to perform the presentation for your class, which is like taking the summative assessment.

In formative assessments, you are either applying learnt knowledge or skills to determine where you are in your learning, get an idea of how a future summative assessment will be formatted, or determining what needs attention before a summative assessment. A formative assessment also has a decided criteria, showing that it is as important as a summative assessment because you might receive a final mark or show you what in your learning needs improvement.

In summative assessments, you are completing a final task which will be marked to give you a final mark. In your summative assessments, you want to ensure that you are taking things more seriously. Everything which was unsuccessful in a formative assessment whether it is making mathematical errors in Criteria A or not completely justifying a chosen design in Criteria B, applying teacher feedback is crucial if you want to see a change in result.

So, when it comes to writing a formative or summative assessment, it is important to understand that both play a crucial role.

MYP5

The MYP eAssessments (Only for MYP5)

MYP eAssessments are external digital assessments that can be taken at the end of the MYP. Instead of a traditional paper exam, eAssessments are completed on a computer. It runs in three ways, depending on the subject.

  • On-screen exams: Language and Literature, Individuals and Societies, Sciences, Mathematics, and Interdisciplinary Learning. The IB marks these
  • ePortfolios: Completed in either Arts, Design, and Physical and Health Education. The Personal Project is also an ePortolio-based subject. Teachers mark these. The IB then moderates them.
  • The Personal Project is also counted in the eAssessments. Teachers mark these. The IB then moderates them.
  • Both: Language Acquisition. Students sit an on-screen exam and also do a speaking assessment that their teacher marks.

At different times of year 5 of the MYP, you will first be completing your ePortfolio’s and Personal Project and later sit the 6 on-screen exams. By taking the MYP eAssessments, you are working towards receiving a certificate.

Grades

When you complete the eAssessments, all of your subjects are marked, scaled to a 1-7, and added together to receive a total mark out of 56.

Example:

Language and Literature (English) - 6
Language Acquisition (German) - 5
Integrated Humanities (Individuals and Societies) - 6
Integrated Sciences (Sciences) - 6
Mathematics - 7
Interdisciplinary - 5
Design ePortfolio - 7
Personal Project - 6
Total points: 48/56

Requirements to Obtain the MYP Certificate

  • Complete six subject eAssessments: Language & Literature, Language Acquisition, Individuals & Societies, Sciences, Mathematics, and One of: Arts, Physical & Health Education, or Design
  • Complete the Interdisciplinary Learning on-screen examination.
  • Complete the Personal Project.
  • Get at least a 3/7 in every required eAssessment component.
  • Get a minimum 28/56 points overall. There are 8 components × 7 points = 56 maximum points.
  • Meet your school's requirements for community service / Service as Action.
  • Participate in the final year of the MYP (MYP 5).
